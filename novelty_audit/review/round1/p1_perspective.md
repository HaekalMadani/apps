## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor). From my seat this means the design, data handling and statistical reporting would survive a referee from a neighbouring quantitative field, psychometrics included. That reader expects reliability and decision quantities to be estimable from the planned sample, uncertainty to be reported with them, and the analysis to be specified well enough that someone else could reproduce it. The methodology seat owns this dimension. I note it only so I can tell where my own concerns about measurement properties stop and its concerns start.

D2 (domain_accuracy). The claims must match current evidence in the fields the work draws on, and borrowed constructs and prior results must be described as their source fields would describe them. For a reader from an adjacent field, a term carried over from another discipline should keep its original meaning or be explicitly redefined. The domain seat owns this dimension.

D3 (argumentative_coherence). The central argument must hold together. The evidence offered should support the claims made, and no reasoning error should undercut the main thesis. Across disciplines this includes whether a conclusion drawn in one field's vocabulary actually follows from evidence gathered under another field's assumptions. The devil's advocate and the methodology seat own this dimension.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance). This is the dimension I own. Readers from adjacent fields should be able to follow the framing, definitions and implications. Any claim that crosses disciplines, especially one that imports measurement-theory or decision-theory concepts into an HCI or motor-learning setting, must be backed by argument or evidence and not by vocabulary alone. In practice I will check three things. First, whether the reliability and decision-consistency language the work uses would be recognised and accepted by someone in that tradition. Second, whether the work keeps the reliability of a metric separate from the reliability of a decision made with that metric. Third, whether its implications reach beyond the immediate application without overreaching.

D5 (writing_and_structure). The manuscript should be well organised, clearly written, and have usable figures and tables that follow venue conventions. For an adjacent-field reader, clarity decides whether the cross-disciplinary argument can be followed at all. The journal-fit seat owns this dimension.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution). The work should fit its target venue and make an original, significant contribution for that venue's readers. No venue is bound for this review, so I make no venue-fit claim. The journal-fit seat owns this dimension.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether imported constructs from reliability theory, generalizability theory and mastery-testing decision consistency are defined, used as their home field uses them, and estimable at the stated sample; whether metric reliability and decision reliability are kept separate; whether falsifiable predictions and the implications for adjacent fields are stated so readers outside the primary field can assess them.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing cross-disciplinary claim rests on a measurement or decision construct that is misapplied or that the stated design cannot estimate, or the reliability of a metric is presented as evidence for the reliability of the decisions made from it, so the central interdisciplinary contribution is unsupported for a reader from that field.
what_triggers_warn: Imported terms are used correctly but are under-defined, unjustified at the planned sample, or missing the standard companion quantities or alternative frames an adjacent-field reader would expect, or implications for other fields are stated more broadly than the evidence supports, without undermining the core claim.
