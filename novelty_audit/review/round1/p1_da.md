## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat): From an adversarial standpoint, this dimension asks whether the design, the handling of data, the statistical reporting and the means offered for reproduction would survive a hostile referee in the field. I will not score it, but I will look for places where a methodological weakness is what makes an argument look stronger than it is, and pass those to the methodology seat rather than scoring them myself.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain seat): This dimension asks whether the claims agree with current evidence in the field, whether prior work is described faithfully, and whether technical terms and reported results are used correctly. The adversarial angle is a cited precedent that does not support what it is cited for, or a borrowed paradigm stretched past what it shows. I do not score this; I note such cases only where they carry weight in the central argument.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by me as devil's advocate and also eligible for the methodology seat): This is my dimension. It asks whether the core thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence offered really supports each claim it is attached to, and whether any fallacy (circular justification, a conclusion that is unfalsifiable as stated, equivocation between a heuristic and a measurement, survivorship in which cases are counted, or a strongest counter-argument left unaddressed) undermines the central argument. My job is to build the strongest case against the thesis and test whether the document has already answered it.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by the perspective seat): This dimension asks whether readers from neighbouring fields can follow the framing, definitions and implications, and whether claims that cross disciplinary lines are backed up. Adversarially, the risk is a concept imported from one field that keeps its name but loses its meaning. I do not score it.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by the editor-in-chief seat): This dimension covers how the document is organised, how clearly it explains itself, the quality of its figures and tables, and whether it follows venue conventions. I do not score it, except to note when unclear exposition hides a gap in the argument.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief seat): This dimension asks whether the work fits the configured venue and makes an original, significant contribution for that readership. Adversarially, the question is whether the claimed contribution is still there once the hedges and limitations are added up. I do not score it, and since no venue criteria are bound for this call I make no claim about venue alignment.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether each central claim is backed by evidence of the right kind and strength; whether the stated limitations, taken together, leave the thesis standing; whether any justification is circular, unfalsifiable, or switches meaning between a heuristic trigger and a measurement; and whether the strongest counter-argument against the core proposal is named and answered.
what_triggers_block: A central claim that depends on evidence that cannot support it (for example, a parameter justified only by a procedure that presupposes it, or a feasibility claim resting on an unvalidated model), or a strongest counter-argument that, if left unanswered, overturns the main recommendation, and which revision could fix.
what_triggers_warn: Secondary claims that go beyond their evidence, hedges that are inconsistent across sections, or a reasonable alternative explanation or design that is mentioned but not dealt with, none of which on its own overturns the core thesis.
what_triggers_fatal: The core thesis contradicts itself, or depends on a logical fallacy so basic that no revision within the current framing could rescue it, so that the central argument fails as stated rather than merely lacking support.
