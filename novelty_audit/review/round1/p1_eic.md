## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by methodology). From an editorial standpoint, this asks whether the design, data handling, statistical reporting and reproducibility support are strong enough to survive peer review in the stated fields. I do not score it. I will read the methodology seat's verdict because a contribution claim only holds if the evidence behind it can be trusted.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by domain). This asks whether the claims match current evidence in the domain, whether prior work is described fairly and correctly, and whether domain terms and reported results are free of error. I do not score it. I treat it as a precondition for any novelty claim, since novelty judged against misrepresented prior work cannot stand.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate, also open to methodology). This asks whether the central thesis is consistent with itself, whether the evidence actually carries each claim, and whether any fallacy undermines the core argument. I do not score it. Editorially, it decides whether the stated contribution is the one the document argues for.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by perspective). This asks whether framing, definitions and implications are readable by people in neighbouring fields, and whether claims that cross disciplines are backed up. I do not score it. It matters to me because the paper names three fields, and a reader from any one of them must be able to follow the parts drawn from the other two.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by eic). This asks whether the document is well organised and clearly written, whether any figures and tables are good, and whether it follows the conventions expected for its genre. Here the genre is a short concept brief written for a methodological review, not a full archival paper. I score this dimension.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by eic). This asks whether the work fits its intended outlet and makes a contribution that is original, significant and right for that readership. No target venue is bound, so I judge fit only against a generic thesis-level and CHI-class HCI standard and make no claim about any specific venue. I score this dimension. It is mandatory, so a fatal finding here leads to rejection.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: A clear order that states the problem, the proposed mechanism, the contribution, the design and the expected results; terms defined before they are used; claims and disclaimers that a reviewer can find quickly; any figure or table readable and referenced in the text; length and density suited to a concept brief written for methodological review.
what_triggers_block: The structure stops a reviewer from working out what is proposed or what is claimed, for example because the central contribution or the research questions cannot be found, or because key terms are used throughout without ever being defined, so the other seats cannot assess the document reliably.
what_triggers_warn: The document can be followed but has fixable problems: uneven section order, jargon or internal shorthand left unexplained for an outside reader, claims and caveats scattered rather than collected, or a figure or table that is unclear or never referenced.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution
dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: A stated contribution an HCI reader would recognise as such, not an analysis method attached to a system after the fact; a novelty claim narrow enough to defend given what the document disclaims; a reportable result even if the expected outcome is null or the study is small; a question whose answer would change what designers of adaptive systems do; fit judged only against a generic thesis or CHI-class standard, since no venue is bound.
what_triggers_block: The contribution as stated is either too broad to defend against what the document itself disclaims, or too thin to report on its own, so it would need substantial re-scoping before it could be reviewed at the target standard. The work is still salvageable.
what_triggers_warn: A defensible contribution exists, but it is under-argued: the novelty boundary is vague, its significance to adaptive-system designers is asserted rather than shown, or what will be reported if the result is null or inconclusive is left unclear.
what_triggers_fatal: No identifiable original contribution remains once the document's own disclaimers are applied, or the work as framed is outside the scope of HCI and adaptive-training research altogether, so no re-scoping could make it reviewable at the target standard.
