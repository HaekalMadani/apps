## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by methodology): Read for domain accuracy, this dimension asks whether the design, data handling, statistical reporting and reproducibility support would satisfy a peer reviewer in the field. From a motor-learning standpoint, that means checking that the measurement schedule (acquisition, retention and transfer phases, and when feedback is present or absent) is described well enough for its methodological soundness to be judged. I do not score this dimension. My concern is only where a design choice misnames a domain construct.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by domain): This is my dimension. The manuscript's claims must agree with the current evidence in augmented-feedback and motor-learning research. Cited prior work must be represented faithfully, without inflating or reversing what it found. Domain terms must be used as the field defines them: concurrent versus terminal feedback, bandwidth KR, the guidance hypothesis, retention and transfer tests, dependency, CE/VE/AE. A construct must not be quietly redefined to fit what the system can measure.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by da with methodology eligible): The central thesis must hold together internally. The evidence offered must actually support the stated claims, and no fallacy may undercut the main argument. Seen through domain accuracy, an argument built on a misread construct or a misattributed finding is incoherent even if its logic is valid. I do not score this dimension.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by perspective): Framing, definitions and implications should be understandable to readers in adjacent fields, and any claim that crosses disciplines should be backed up. For my field, the question is whether motor-learning terms are defined well enough that HCI or psychometrics readers will not take them in a non-standard sense. I do not score this dimension.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by eic): This covers organisation, clarity of exposition, figures and tables, and adherence to venue conventions. From a domain viewpoint, clear exposition includes stating which feedback was present in each phase so that a reader can tell what a test measured. I do not score this dimension.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by eic): The work must fit the configured venue and make an original, significant contribution for that readership. In domain terms, a contribution claimed against the feedback literature has to be genuinely new relative to that literature, not a renaming of established findings. I do not score this dimension and make no venue-fit claim.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether feedback-removal tests are correctly classed as retention, transfer or neither given the delay and what changes between phases; whether a dependency or guidance-hypothesis construct is defined as the literature defines it (performance degradation on feedback withdrawal after acquisition with that feedback); whether bandwidth-KR, concurrent-versus-terminal and CE/VE/AE terms are used correctly; whether known withdrawal effects (immediate post-removal performance dip, surprise, attentional reorientation) are acknowledged as rival explanations; whether withdrawing multiple feedback sources together is accounted for; and whether every cited finding is represented with its actual direction and scope.
what_triggers_block: A central construct such as retention, transfer or guidance dependency is used in a sense the field does not recognise, or a load-bearing citation is misrepresented, in a way that changes how the main result would be interpreted but could be corrected by reframing without redesigning the study.
what_triggers_warn: Peripheral terminology is imprecise, a relevant rival account from the feedback-removal literature goes unmentioned, or a citation is stated more broadly or confidently than its source supports, without affecting the central claim.
what_triggers_fatal: The core claim depends on a construct redefined to fit the measurement, or on a prior finding reported in the opposite direction from the source, so that no reframing could rescue the central conclusion without changing the study itself.
